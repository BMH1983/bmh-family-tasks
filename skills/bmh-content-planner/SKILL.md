# BMH Content Planner Skill

Turns Sandra's Sunday newsletter plus her weekly content dump into a complete Monday to Friday content plan. Two inputs, one plan, both platforms, all done on Monday.

Read the `bmh-voice` skill before planning anything.

---

## The Line This Skill Does Not Cross

**Ruling (3 August 2026).** The Week of 20 Jul plan shipped finished captions, finished LinkedIn posts, and cold-invented Reel hooks ("You've been told AI is a tech skill. It's not."), then stamped itself "voice checked (confirmed)". Sandra read one hook and wanted to close the laptop. Diagnosis: this skill did bmh-caption-writer's job without bmh-caption-writer's rules. That is never allowed to happen again.

**This skill produces PLANS, not copy.** Angles, formats, sources, one-line directions. Nothing this skill outputs is postable. Five hard rules:

1. **No finished captions. No finished LinkedIn posts.** The output template below is the ceiling: hook, brief, slide list, one-line caption direction, one-line CTA direction. If you find yourself writing a full caption or a full post, stop. That is bmh-caption-writer's job, behind its quality check.
2. **Every hook must declare its provenance.** A hook is either Sandra's words lifted verbatim from the newsletter or dump (mark it: `Sandra verbatim`), or a one-line description of what the hook needs to do (mark it: `NEEDS SANDRA'S WORDS`). Never write a finished hook line cold. Cold-invented hooks default to second-person lecture openers ("You've been told...", "You are not...") and copywriter fragments, which is exactly what Sandra's ear catches. Her voice bank contains zero second-person lecture openers. Banned in this skill entirely, including examples.
3. **Never self-certify voice.** No "voice check" section, no "confirmed", no claiming anything passes the non-negotiables. Only Sandra rules on voice (bmh-voice, First Rule). The plan ends with next steps, not a certificate.
4. **An empty or stale dump changes the plan's status.** If the dump has nothing from the last 7 days, say so in the first line of the plan, seed only from the newsletter, and stamp every day `BRIEF ONLY, no Sandra material beyond the newsletter`. Do not stretch one newsletter into five days of finished-sounding content. Fewer, honest days beat five padded ones.
5. **Never recycle Voice Bank lines into planned posts.** The bank calibrates rhythm. The stories in it are hers and they are used. If a bank line appears verbatim in a plan, that is a defect, not voice matching.

## The Two Inputs

**Input 1: The newsletter**
Sandra's Sunday email. Provides 2 to 3 posts maximum. Do not force the newsletter into more days than it naturally fits.

**Input 2: The weekly content dump**
Raw moments, quotes, stories, opinions, and event content Sandra has collected throughout the week in her BMH Content Dump project. Fills the remaining days.

**The rule:** Newsletter seeds the week. Real life fills it. Never force content from one source to stretch further than it naturally goes.

If either input is missing, ask for it before building the plan.

## The Four Content Pillars

Every post lives inside one of these.

**1. She Feels Seen**
The mental load, isolation, truth of doing business while mothering. Makes her text a friend. Highest DM share potential.

**2. In the Room**
Real stories, real moments, real women from Open Coffee and Village Circles. Photo-led, specific, named. Proof the village is real.

**3. Sandra's Take**
Sandra's opinion on business, community, money, success. Short, punchy, said plainly. Makes people follow a person not a brand.

**4. Come Learn Claude**
The invitation. Open Coffee dates, Village Circles spots, intensive seats. Warm, specific, never salesy. One mention per week maximum. "Table" never appears in copy.

**The single rule:** Every post either makes her feel seen or makes her want to be in the room.

## Choosing the Format

Format is decided by the content, not by the day of the week.

**Look at what the content actually is, then ask:**

- Is this a strong opinion or a punchy take? → **Reel** (Sandra talking to camera, 30 to 90 seconds)
- Is this a story with multiple beats or a list with real depth? → **Carousel** (8 to 10 slides)
- Is this a single powerful moment or image? → **Single image post**
- Is this a one-liner that deserves its own frame? → **Quote graphic**
- Is this a behind-the-scenes or day-in-the-life moment? → **Stories**

**Algorithm guidance to inform the decision (not to override the content):**

- Reels reach non-followers. Use when the goal is discovery and growing the audience.
- Carousels deepen engagement with existing followers. Use for saves and shares. Instagram re-serves carousels — aim for 8 to 10 slides.
- Single images work for strong visual moments but get the least algorithmic push.
- DM shares are the strongest signal. Whatever format, plan for shareability.
- Reel hooks decide distribution. First 3 seconds determine whether Instagram pushes it wider.
- 5 quality posts per week beats daily weak content.

**Across the week aim for variety** — ideally 2 Reels, 2 carousels, 1 single image or quote graphic. But if the content says otherwise, follow the content.

## Two Platforms, One Voice

Every post gets an Instagram version and a LinkedIn version. Same story. Same Sandra. Different format.

**Instagram:**
- Captions truncate at ~125 characters — first line must earn the "more" tap
- Keywords woven naturally into caption for SEO — this is how Instagram finds and distributes your content now
- No hashtags needed — posts without hashtags are outperforming hashtag-heavy posts in 2026
- Link in bio for CTAs
- Shorter, punchier, more visual

**LinkedIn:**
- No truncation games — the full story can be told
- No hashtag strategy needed
- Link always in first comment, never the post body
- Longer posts perform well — 150 to 400 words for story-led content
- Same voice, no professionalising, no corporate polish
- Opinion posts and raw storytelling perform best
- No carousel format — if carousel content, write as a long-form post instead

**The non-negotiable:** Sandra's voice does not change between platforms. LinkedIn gets more room, not a different person.

---

## What the Algorithm Rewards in 2026

**DM shares are the strongest signal on Instagram.** Before locking any post, ask: would someone DM this to a specific friend?

**Watch time decides Reel distribution.** Weak hook in first 3 seconds = dead Reel.

**Carousels get a second chance.** Instagram re-serves carousels to people who didn't swipe. More slides = more chances.

**Keywords beat hashtags.** Instagram now reads caption text like a search engine. Weave natural language keywords into the caption — mums in business, Adelaide, accountability, body doubling, community. Do not use hashtags. Research shows posts without hashtags are outperforming hashtag-heavy posts in 2026.

**Stories maintain relationships.** Post 3 to 7 per day. Interactive stickers (polls, questions) boost engagement.

**LinkedIn rewards dwell time.** Long-form storytelling and opinion posts that make people stop and read perform best.

## Hook Rules

**Reel hooks — first 3 seconds:**
Say the most interesting thing first. No slow intros. No "hey guys." And per the rules at the top: a hook in the plan is Sandra's words verbatim or a NEEDS SANDRA'S WORDS direction. The examples below are calibration for what a hook does, not licence to write one cold.

Good (from Sandra's real voice):
- "The thing nobody tells you about running a business from home."
- "I spent six months growing a weed."
- "Fine is not the same as not struggling."
- "You cannot do this alone. I tried."

Bad:
- "Hey everyone, today I want to talk about..."
- "So I've been thinking a lot lately about..."

**Carousel hooks — slide 1:**
Specific, surprising, or names a feeling. Must make someone swipe.

**LinkedIn opening line:**
No truncation pressure but still needs to hook. Can build slightly slower but must earn the scroll-stop within the first two lines.

## Shareability Check

For every Instagram post: would someone DM this to a specific friend?
For every LinkedIn post: would someone tag someone in the comments or share to their network?

Most shareable content:
- Makes her laugh in recognition
- Says something true nobody else is saying
- Shows a real moment from inside the room
- Validates something she's been feeling alone about

If a post doesn't pass, find the angle that would before locking it in.
